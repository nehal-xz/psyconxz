Psychology Theories

Explore established frameworks that illuminate how human beings think, cope, and grow.

Cognitive Behavioral Theory (CBT)

Aaron Beck · 1960s

Our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors form an active loop. By gently identifying and reframing automatic negative thought patterns, we can reduce emotional distress and discover balanced ways of living.

Hierarchy of Needs

Abraham Maslow · 1943

Human motivation flows upward across tiers: physiological needs (rest, nourishment), safety, love and belonging, self-esteem, and self-actualization. When foundational needs are strained, emotional well-being naturally feels depleted.

Attachment Theory

John Bowlby · 1958

The emotional bonds formed in our earliest relationships shape our internal working models for closeness, vulnerability, and trust throughout adult life and during times of interpersonal stress.

Self-Determination Theory

Edward Deci & Richard Ryan · 1985

People thrive and maintain vitality when three fundamental psychological needs are fulfilled: Autonomy (having a voice), Competence (feeling effective), and Relatedness (feeling valued by others).

Learned Helplessness

Martin Seligman · 1967

When people endure prolonged stress where they feel powerless, they may subconsciously learn that their efforts do not matter. Recognizing this pattern is the first step toward reclaiming personal agency.

Stress and Coping Theory

Richard Lazarus & Susan Folkman · 1984

Stress is not merely an external event, but the appraisal between the challenge at hand and the internal resources we feel we possess. Balanced coping blends problem-solving with emotional self-soothing.

ABC Model of REBT

Albert Ellis · 1955

It is not the Activating event (A) itself that causes our emotional Consequences (C), but the internal Beliefs (B) we hold about that event. Questioning rigid 'shoulds' brings emotional relief.

Person-Centered Theory

Carl Rogers · 1951

Every person possesses an innate tendency toward growth and self-healing. When provided an environment of unconditional positive regard, genuine empathy, and non-judgment, we can find our own clarity.

Logotherapy

Viktor Frankl · 1946

Finding purpose and meaning in life — even in the face of suffering and adversity — is the deepest human drive. Meaning can be discovered through creative work, authentic love, and courageous attitudes.

Self-Efficacy Theory

Albert Bandura · 1977

Our confidence in our ability to execute specific actions strongly influences how we think, motivate ourselves, and persevere. Small, gradual mastery experiences rebuild faith in ourselves.

About psyconxz

Compassionate psychology education, grounded in science and empathy.

Our Purpose

psyconxz was built on the belief that psychological frameworks shouldn't stay locked inside textbooks. When people understand the mechanics behind their feelings, the weight of self-blame lifts, and clarity begins to emerge.

How It Works

1. Observation Phase: We ask 7 gentle questions covering your emotions, triggers, sleep, energy, and coping style.

2. Collaborative Verification: We summarize what was shared to ensure you feel truly heard.

3. Theory Matching: We introduce the established psychological theory that matches your situation, explaining its origin, core idea, why it fits you, and 3 to 5 realistic daily steps.

psyconxz is an educational companion, NOT a medical provider or therapist. It does not diagnose clinical conditions, prescribe medications, or replace professional mental health care.
